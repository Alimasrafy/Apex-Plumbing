Essential Water Heater Maintenance Tips

Build a safer maintenance routine around your equipment—not guesswork.

Apex Plumbing editorial · October 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Reliable hot water begins with safe equipment and a maintenance plan that matches it. These principles help you prepare for a professional visit without attempting hazardous work.

Why equipment-specific maintenance matters

Water heaters differ in energy source, design, controls, and safety requirements. Start with the manual for your exact model. A storage tank, tankless unit, and heat-pump water heater do not share a single maintenance procedure. Keep model details and service records together so a technician can prepare appropriately.

Recognize changes in performance

Reduced capacity, delayed hot water, unusual noise, or inconsistent temperature can have different causes. Record what changed and when. Water near a tank can originate at a fitting or the tank itself. A leaking tank typically needs replacement, but the source should be identified rather than assumed.

Keep access and ventilation clear

Do not store belongings against the equipment or obstruct required clearances, vents, intake screens, controls, or service access. Follow the manufacturer’s requirements for the specific installation. Never place combustible materials near gas equipment. Heat-pump models need appropriate airflow and room conditions.

Arrange appropriate professional maintenance

Ask a qualified professional what your manufacturer recommends for flushing, descaling, anode inspection, filters, and safety-component checks. Water conditions affect maintenance needs. Draining a neglected tank or disturbing a corroded fitting can create problems, so a blanket DIY flushing schedule is not responsible advice.

Signs that call for professional service

Persistent leakage, visible corrosion, scorch marks, repeated ignition faults, or unreliable heating need an assessment. Do not bypass controls or repeatedly reset a unit that shuts down. For a gas smell, leave immediately and call your utility or 911 from a safe location. Carbon monoxide alarms are not a substitute for safe installation and maintenance.

Hot-water safety comes first

Hot water and relief discharge can cause serious burns. Never cap, plug, or obstruct a temperature-and-pressure relief outlet. Do not test safety components without the correct procedure and precautions. Ask a professional about safe delivery temperatures, mixing valves, and household scald risks while accounting for local health and code guidance.

Plan replacement rather than rushing it

If repair is no longer sensible, compare capacity, energy use, installation requirements, warranty, and total installed cost. Tankless and heat-pump systems may need changes to gas, electrical, venting, or space provision. Confirm permits and approved disposal arrangements before the work is authorized.
