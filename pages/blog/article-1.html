Common Plumbing Problems Every Homeowner Should Know

Know what to look for, what you can check, and when it’s time to call a professional.

Apex Plumbing editorial · October 16, 2026 · 7 min read

You do not need to be a plumber to notice when something changes. The useful skill is knowing which observations help, which actions are safe, and when to stop and ask for qualified help.

Start with the symptoms

A slow drain, a dripping tap, and a damp ceiling may look unrelated, but each is a reason to pause and observe. Write down when the symptom appears, which fixtures are affected, and whether anything recently changed. A short history helps a professional distinguish an isolated fixture issue from a wider system problem.

A tap that will not stop dripping

A worn cartridge, washer, or valve seat may allow water through even when the tap is closed. If you can safely use the fixture’s shutoff, isolate it before inspecting an accessible external connection. Do not force a seized valve. Identify the tap model so the correct repair part can be selected.

A toilet that runs between flushes

Listen for refilling when nobody has used the toilet. Tank water may be escaping into the bowl through a worn seal, or a fill valve may be set incorrectly. Do not overtighten ceramic fittings. Cracked porcelain and water at the base need prompt assessment rather than an improvised patch.

Slow drains and recurring clogs

A single slow basin may have a local restriction. Several fixtures backing up together can indicate a shared line problem. Avoid chemical drain cleaners, and never add another product to a drain that already contains chemicals. Stop using water if wastewater is coming back into the building.

Low flow and unexpected pressure changes

First compare hot and cold water and check whether the issue affects one fixture or the whole property. A clogged aerator can reduce flow at one tap; widespread changes may involve a valve, regulator, utility issue, or concealed leak. Do not adjust a pressure regulator without understanding the system.

When to call a professional

Get urgent help for uncontrolled leakage, sewage entering occupied space, or water near electricity. Never enter standing water near electrical equipment. If you smell gas, leave immediately and call your utility or 911 from a safe location. Repeated small symptoms also deserve attention before damage spreads.

A manageable maintenance routine

Keep the area around water heaters and shutoffs accessible. Check visible connections for moisture, use drain strainers, and review your bill for unexplained changes. Record repairs and retain fixture manuals. Preventive care supports early detection; it does not guarantee that a system will never fail.
